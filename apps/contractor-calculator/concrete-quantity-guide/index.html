How much concrete should you order?

Follow a rectangular slab from dimensions to volume, waste allowance and a rounded order quantity.

This example uses a slab 12 feet long, 10 feet wide and 4 inches thick. It illustrates material arithmetic; it does not determine whether that slab design is suitable for your site.

1. Calculate the base volume

Convert thickness to feet: 4 ÷ 12 = ⅓ foot. Multiply length × width × thickness.

12 × 10 × ⅓ = 40 cubic feet
40 ÷ 27 ≈ 1.4815 cubic yards

2. Add your chosen allowance

For this example, a 10% allowance gives 40 × 1.10 = 44 cubic feet, or approximately 1.6296 cubic yards. An allowance is an input to your estimate, not a universal requirement. Confirm it with your supplier.

3. Round to the ordering increment

With a supplier order increment of 0.25 cubic yards, round 1.6296 upward to 1.75 cubic yards. Confirm your supplier’s actual increment before ordering.

If your supplier price is $185 per cubic yard, the rounded order costs 1.75 × $185 = $323.75 for material. Labor, delivery and taxes are excluded.

Using bags instead?

Choose one material source; do not add bag quantities to the ready-mix order. For the same 44 cubic feet:

  • At 0.45 cubic feet per 60 lb bag: round 44 ÷ 0.45 upward to 98 bags.
  • At 0.60 cubic feet per 80 lb bag: round 44 ÷ 0.60 upward to 74 bags.

Use the stated yield on the exact product you intend to buy.

Before ordering
